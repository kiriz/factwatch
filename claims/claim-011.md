## Claim Text

"MSG (monosodium glutamate) causes headaches, numbness, and other symptoms — so-called 'Chinese restaurant syndrome.'"

## Context

The term "Chinese restaurant syndrome" was coined in a 1968 letter to the New England Journal of Medicine. It became a widespread belief that led to MSG being banned from many foods and restaurants. The FDA and most modern research have a different conclusion.

## Verification Notes (Human)

Check FDA classification of MSG (GRAS status). Look for double-blind studies on MSG sensitivity. Verify whether glutamate in MSG differs from glutamate naturally present in tomatoes, cheese, and mushrooms. Check the original 1968 NEJM letter and subsequent research.
